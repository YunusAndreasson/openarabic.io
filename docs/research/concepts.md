# Three concepts for the story page

Written 2026-10-31 for step 5 of the foundation plan. Specimens are in `docs/specimens/concept-a/`, `concept-b/` and `concept-c/`. Open them through any local static server, because the fonts need same-origin requests. They hold no script.

**What this is:** an agent's reasoning and browser checks. No learner has seen any of this. The critiques below are judgement, and the emotion test in step 7 is still the only evidence that can count.

**Fixed for all three:**

- The moment belongs to the visitor: reading a classical line without the English. No founder story.
- The opposing force is the seductive idea that a translation is enough and the original is out of reach. The page argues for reaching the original, never against translations. The app itself shows English beside the Arabic.
- Native scroll, no pin on phones, dark everywhere. All three specimens use no pin at any size, so the one allowed on a laptop stays unspent.
- Reviewer: the `film-director` method adapted, without its "moral outrage" and "injustice" bar.

## A. One sentence

**The idea:** one line from Ibn al-Jawzi, about finding a book. The visitor meets it unread, learns it with their own taps, removes the English themselves, and meets it again.

| Screen | On the screen |
|---|---|
| 1 | The line, 9 tappable words, no English. "Ibn al-Jawzi, Ṣayd al-Khāṭir". "OpenArabic: Read the Classics". "If you can sound out Arabic script, you can start reading classical texts." "Free. No ads. No subscription." Store links |
| 2 | "One tap gives you the meaning, a grammar note and audio." Real capture of the word card |
| 3 | "Colour shows how multi-part words are built." فَكَأَنِّي, very large, in three colours, with "so as if I" coloured to match |
| 4 | "Arabic audio on every word and every line." The line's real audio, played only on a tap. "Every text plays English first, then Arabic, or Arabic only." AI voice disclosure |
| 5 | "English above every Arabic line." Real capture of the reader. Machine-translation disclosure |
| 6 | "You answer out loud before the app shows the meaning." "Words return when your predicted recall falls to 90%." Real capture of voice review |
| 7 | "Excerpts from 22 classical works by six scholars". The six names. "Sorted by difficulty. Most texts are advanced; 48 are easier." |
| 8 | The line with its English sentence above and each word's English beneath. One control: "Take the English away" |
| 9 | The line exactly as on screen 1. Nothing explains it. Then: "That was this page. In the app, the English stays above the line." Store links, account note, sadaqah draft |

- **One remembered image:** the same line, twice, looking identical. Only the reader has changed.
- **Set-up and pay-off:** the set-up is the first line of the page, which the visitor can sound out and can't yet read. The pay-off is that line on screen 9, or earlier if they press the control. The line's last word is كَنْزٍ, "a treasure", so the pay-off lands on the word the sentence was reaching for.
- **First real app UI:** the tappable line on screen 1 uses the app's published word data but isn't the app. The first capture is screen 2.
- **The ending stays honest:** the screen says at once that this moment was the page and that the app keeps its English. The control can't be read as an Arabic-only reader.
- **Imagery:** one generated scene with no lettering at screen 7, such as a lamplit shelf of closed books with no people, and one promo shot at screen 5 with capture 01 composited in. Screens 1, 8 and 9 stay image-free, because the Arabic is the picture there.

**Honest critique**

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| **Rams** | Strong. The line does the work of a hero image, a demo and an ending. Screen 7 could be challenged, but it carries the honesty about difficulty. Screen 3's giant word is the one indulgence; it earns its place because the colour can be read at that size. Weak spot: 9 tappable words plus 18 cards is a lot of markup for one sentence |
| **Norman** | Visceral: strong, the script is the first thing seen. Behavioural: a tap answers at once, and it works with no script. Reflective: "I can read this" is the goal, but the visitor has only learned nine words, and a careful reader may feel the trick. **The underline may not signal "tap me" (D1), and only people can test that** |
| **Nielsen** | First screen passes N1 at all three sizes in both browsers. Screens 8 and 9 run over 40 words (about 50 and 43). On a laptop the card opens centred below the word's line, not always next to the word, which only partly meets D3. Word cards have an explicit Close and light dismiss |
| **Nolan** | Best of the three. Structure carries the meaning, there's one reveal, and the visitor performs it. Real text and real screens. **Risk:** the pay-off is quiet. If nobody feels the second appearance, the page has no climax, and only cold viewing can tell |
| **Against the brief** | Pressing a control to remove English could read as anti-translation. The line beside it says the app keeps English, which answers that, but test the words |

## B. A thousand years

**The idea:** time as the structure. A vertical rule between lines is drawn to scale, one length per year, and runs from al-Bukhari through to the app's version number, which is the current Hijri year. اقْرَأْ is not used.

| Screen | On the screen |
|---|---|
| 1 | "256", the line from al-Bukhari, 7 tappable words, the name of the work, product name, requirement, price, store links |
| 2 | A rule for 341 years. "597". The Ibn al-Jawzi line with English. "One tap gives you the meaning, a grammar note and audio." |
| 3 | A rule for 131 years. "728". The Ibn Taymiyyah line with English. "Arabic audio on every word and every line." |
| 4 | A rule for 23 years. "751". The first line of al-Fawaid with English. "Excerpts from 22 classical works by six scholars" |
| 5 | The longest rule, 697 years. "1448", very large. "The version number of the app is this year, in the Hijri calendar. Today it reads 1448.4.3 on the App Store." |
| 6 | "English above every Arabic line." Real capture of the reader. Disclosures |
| 7 | The al-Bukhari line again, no English. Store links, account note, sadaqah draft |

- **One remembered image:** the long empty rule ending at "1448".
- **Set-up and pay-off:** years beside lines look like dates. The pay-off is that the app's own version number is the next date.
- **First real app UI:** the capture on screen 6, late. The tappable line on screen 1 is published data, not the app.
- **Imagery:** one generated scene per era, with no lettering and no people. This is the hungriest concept for imagery and the most likely to look like a museum.

**Honest critique**

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| **Rams** | The rule to scale is honest and cheap. The years themselves don't help the visitor do anything |
| **Norman** | Visceral: strong. Behavioural: same as A. Reflective: the feeling is awe at the tradition, not "I can read this". **It gives the moment to history, not to the visitor,** which goes against the owner's decision |
| **Nielsen** | N1 passes. A learner who doesn't know the Hijri calendar has to work out what the numbers mean, against "nothing on the first screen has to be learned" (C1). The death years aren't in the ledger, so they need owner approval |
| **Nolan** | The most Nolan on the surface, with time as the structure. The reveal is clever rather than felt: a version number is a detail for people who already care. Reads like a puzzle, and he says it shouldn't be |
| **Honesty** | Placing an app at the end of a line of scholars makes a claim of succession that nothing supports. 1448 minus 256 is 1,192 Hijri years, so "a thousand" is fair as a round figure, but only because al-Bukhari opens it. **The strongest objection is tone: it risks sounding grand about an app** |

## C. The first year

**The idea:** one word, القَلْبِ, "the heart", returns in different real lines, while a bar grows from the Arabic margin to the English margin through the app's own horizon labels, until the word turns up in the first line of al-Fawaid.

| Screen | On the screen |
|---|---|
| 1 | The line from Ibn al-Qayyim on patience, 7 tappable words, the name of the work, product name, requirement, price, store links |
| 2 | "Tap for the meaning. Long-press to save it." The word, large, "the heart". Short bar, "days". Note that the bars are an illustration |
| 3 | "You choose 10, 20 or 30 new words a day." Real capture of the memory horizon, labelled as test-account numbers |
| 4 | Bar at "weeks". A line from Ibn al-Jawzi with the word underlined. "You answer out loud before the app shows the meaning." |
| 5 | Bar at "months". A line from Ibn al-Qayyim with the word underlined. "Words return when your predicted recall falls to 90%." |
| 6 | Full bar, "a year+". The first line of al-Fawaid with the word underlined. The PDF: side by side, 411 pages · 18 MB, free to share. Store links, account note, sadaqah draft |

- **One remembered image:** the bar reaching across the screen and arriving at one known word in a real book.
- **Set-up and pay-off:** a word saved on screen 2, recognised in al-Fawaid on screen 6.
- **First real app UI:** the capture on screen 3, which is the earliest real proof of the most distinctive true claim.
- **Imagery:** the same desk at four light levels, no lettering and no people. Risky, because it pushes towards a time-lapse cliché.

**Honest critique**

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| **Rams** | Shows the true thing nobody else claims. The bar is simple and earns its place |
| **Norman** | Behavioural: good. Reflective: "I could be someone who keeps words". But a visitor reads only one word of the final line, so the moment the owner named doesn't happen on this page |
| **Nielsen** | N1 passes. The test account's capture shows 0 words at "a year+", which undercuts the ending if anyone looks closely. A learner may also take the bars for a measured result, even with the note |
| **Nolan** | Weakest reveal: recognising one word is small, and the page asks the visitor to imagine a year instead of doing anything |
| **Honesty** | **The serious problem.** The structure implies progress by a date, close to the banned "progress in weeks" and R6's "promises of fluency by a date". No data shows how long a horizon takes to grow for a real learner. The illustration note admits this, which also takes the power out of the concept |

**Could anything beat these?** I tried two others on paper. "The book": start from Ibn al-Jawzi's whole passage on reading, which is a stronger text but too long for a first screen. It collapses into A. "The gap": a list of what the visitor can already read, which needs guesses about the visitor and shames them at the point N1 says to be strict. Neither beats A.

## Problems found in the published content

For the owner. None of these changed the specimens beyond avoiding the lines concerned.

- **Texts that read as modern, under a classical attribution.** Article 289, "On Sincerity", labelled Ṣayd al-Khāṭir, has a compass needle (1796), a muscle to be trained (1803) and "three minutes for accountability" (1801). Sentence 2008, labelled Ibn Taymiyyah, al-Kalim al-Ṭayyib, has "screens and appointments". Avoid these as featured text. The site must not imply they are the scholar's own words without checking.
- **Article 559 is mostly a biographer's voice.** It speaks about Ibn al-Jawzi and quotes him. Sentence 7106 is in his first person. From memory it matches the book, but I didn't check an edition.
- **Possible wrong work.** From memory, the musk passage in article 318, "The Meaning of Fasting", is from al-Wābil al-Ṣayyib, not Ighāthat al-Lahfān as published. Capture 01 shows this article, and its caption names the published work. Unverified. Check before using that capture.
- **Sentence text and word data disagree.** 7106 has كِتَابًا where its word row has كتابًا. 7114 has بَعْدُ where its word row has بِعَدٍّ, which looks wrong. 4188 and 2401 are partly unvocalised in the sentence and vocalised in the word rows. The specimens show the sentence as stored.

**What differed by browser:**

- **Zen** in headless mode has a 12 px classic scrollbar, so a 390 px window lays out at 378 px. That is tighter than a phone and everything still fits. The card sits about 6 px off centre as a result.
- **The audio control** is a grey pill in Chromium and a flat bar in Zen.
- **Laptop cards:** the same placement in both, below the word's line and centred on the page.

**Arabic, checked by eye in both:** joined letters hold across the coloured spans in فَكَأَنِّي, رَأَيْتُ, أَرَهُ and وَقَعْتُ. Vowel marks sit on their letters, and the shadda with kasra on نِّ stays clear. The ligature in كِتَابًا is intact. The kasra under إ in the first word comes close to the word's underline at phone size, which should be looked at in the real build.

**Not verified:**

- Any real iPhone, any Safari, and the one-colour path below iOS 26.2. Neither browser takes that path. It's gated in CSS on `field-sizing`, a change from the script in `platform-2026.md`, so it works with no JS, but it has never been seen working.
- No `2026-10-31-spike-word-tap.md` had appeared when this was written, so the mechanics come from `platform-2026.md` alone.
- Selecting and copying Arabic inside word buttons.
- Audio: metadata loads in Chromium at 6 seconds. It wasn't played, nobody listened, and the male-voice rule (C7) is unchecked.
- Zen with the owner's own profile and extensions. LCP, CLS, INP, 200% zoom, an accessibility scan and the N6 40% blank-gap sampling, which I judged by eye only. Daylight reading on a dark page, which the owner review makes a test item.
- Captures are from dev build 1448.5.5, 120 commits behind `main`. Their README asks for a check against 1448.5.9 before publishing.
